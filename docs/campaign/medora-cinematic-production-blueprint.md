# MEDORA Health Care Eco System — Cinematic Campaign Blueprint

## Creative lock

The only project name used in narration, title cards, subtitles, prompts, metadata, and end cards is **MEDORA Health Care Eco System**. Do not use any previous name. The film presents a realistic, premium healthcare operations ecosystem rather than a slideshow. Every shot must contain visible motion, motivated camera movement, real human action, or a meaningful interface transition.

The master film is designed at approximately **150 seconds in 16:9**, with an approved **90-second 9:16 cutdown** for Facebook, WhatsApp, and Instagram. The same characters, facilities, color language, interface logic, and sound identity must be preserved across both versions. The audience includes pharmacies, hospitals and clinics, distributors and warehouses, laboratories, insurers and care organizations, branch managers, finance leaders, operations teams, clinicians, pharmacists, and customers.

## Visual direction

The visual language is cinematic live-action healthcare technology: neutral international professional environments, diverse adult staff, contemporary pharmacy and hospital interiors, clean distribution warehouses, documentary-grade human performances, controlled shallow depth of field, realistic screens and scanners, and restrained holographic overlays. No Gulf clothing, no national stereotypes, no fabricated testimonials, no patient diagnosis claims, no generic stock-photo montage, and no readable invented government certification. GAHAR, e-invoicing, Data Matrix, and regulatory readiness are shown as controlled workflow capabilities or readiness interfaces, not as a claim of official accreditation.

Palette: deep medical navy, graphite, clean white, cool cyan data light, and a restrained MEDORA teal accent. Lighting begins with dawn-blue operational calm, grows into crisp daylight and cyan data energy, peaks in a confident full-spectrum control-room glow, and resolves into a quiet warm-white brand end card. Motion graphics are volumetric but physically grounded: scanner rays, trace paths, node connections, data-grid parallax, clean UI morphs, and particle streams that follow real objects.

## Voice and sound

Narrator: male, Arabic, Modern Standard Arabic with a calm pan-regional professional delivery, warm low-mid register, authoritative but human, documentary pace with deliberate pauses. Recommended voice profile: informative/smooth male. Generate narration per span, not as one uncontrolled block. Keep narration clearly above the music and preserve all diegetic sound.

Music: instrumental cinematic technology score, 82 BPM, D minor moving toward D major color at the resolve, piano ostinato, warm low strings, restrained electronic pulse, glassy synth texture, subtle frame drums without regional styling, wide stereo image, clean premium mix, no vocals. Sound design includes pharmacy ambience, scanner confirmation, carton movement, soft keyboard clicks, distant hospital room tone, warehouse wheels, controlled data pulses, and a single restrained impact at the MEDORA reveal.

## 9:16 adaptation

Recompose every shot rather than crop blindly. Keep people and primary UI action centered in the 9:16 safe column, use larger UI elements, place subtitles above the platform interface zone, and preserve the MEDORA logo with generous top and bottom margin. The approved 90-second cut selects the strongest shots in this order: 00:00–00:08, 00:16–00:32, 00:32–00:48, 00:48–01:04, 01:04–01:20, 01:20–01:36, 01:36–01:52, 01:52–02:08, and 02:16–02:30, with bridging narration rewritten for vertical pacing.

## Post-production and export

Master: 3840×2160, 16:9, 23.976 or 25 fps, 10-bit where available, Rec.709, ProRes 422 HQ or DNxHR HQX master. Delivery H.264/H.265 at high bitrate, AAC 48 kHz, loudness target around -14 LUFS integrated for social delivery while retaining headroom. Vertical: 2160×3840 master and 1080×1920 social delivery, same frame rate and audio sample rate. Burned Arabic subtitles are required for social versions, with separate SRT and VTT files. Keep all text within platform-safe margins and perform a final Arabic spelling and diacritic review.
